# Art provenance

Every image in this directory, where it came from, and what its licence
permits.

## sprites.png and characters.png

- **Source:** this repository. Both sheets are generated by `gen_art.py` in
  this directory, which contains every pixel as a hand-authored map; run
  `uv run --with pillow gen_art.py` to reproduce them byte-for-byte.
- **Style:** original pixel art in the visual grammar of late-SNES-lifecycle JRPG
  interiors (Chrono Trigger, Terranigma, the DQ3 remake, Suikoden): dark warm outlines instead of black,
  four-tone ramps off one master palette, top-left directional light,
  dithered texture, translucent cast shadows, chunky chibi proportions. Style is not copyrightable;
  pixels are, and none of these pixels are taken from any game or asset
  pack.
- **Licence:** same as the repository. No third-party licence attaches.
- **Layout:**
  - `sprites.png`, 112x32: a 7x2 grid of 16x16 tiles in `gen_art.py`'s
    build order — floor, wall, plant, bin, shelf, cooler, dark floor (the
    room checkers the two floor tones) / sofa-left, sofa-right, desk (with
    terminal), chair, picture, door. Furniture carries translucent cast
    shadows, so it must be drawn over the floor. `app.js`'s `S_*` constants
    index this grid.
  - `characters.png`, 192x144: nine rows of twelve 16x16 frames. Rows 0-7
    are the agent robots (eight palette ramps, four head variants), row 8
    is the janitor (grey ramp, work cap). Each row is four walk frames for
    each of down, up, left; right is left mirrored at draw time.

## History

Earlier versions of this view assembled its art from three CC0 packs:
Kenney's RPG Urban Pack and Roguelike Indoors for the tiles, and Pixel-boy's
Ninja Adventure characters (Sparklin Labs). All three are CC0, owed nothing,
and served well — but three packs meant three art styles in one room. They
were replaced wholesale by the generated sheets above so the view has one
consistent style, robot workers that look like what clanker's agents are,
and art the repository owns outright. Thanks to Kenney
(<https://kenney.nl>) and Pixel-boy (<http://superpowers-html5.com/>)
anyway: CC0 asks for nothing, which is exactly why they deserve the credit.

## Rejected along the way, and why

Checked and not used, so nobody re-treads it:

- **Ripping actual SNES-era sprites** (Chrono Trigger, Terranigma, Dragon
  Quest): the brief names those games as the aesthetic bar, but their
  pixels are copyrighted. The style was studied; nothing was copied.
- **Pixel Office 32x32** (masalimov-ilnur): closest fit by subject, but paid
  and its licence forbids redistribution of the asset files - vendoring it
  here is exactly that.
- **Mystic Woods** (free version): non-commercial only, no redistribution.
- **16x16 RPG characters** (CC BY-SA 3.0): usable, but share-alike puts a
  copyleft obligation on the art where generating our own puts none.
